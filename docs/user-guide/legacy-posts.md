# How do I review the old blog posts?

The site before WordPress (2015 to 2023) carried about 127 blog posts that never made the move. They were recovered from the Wayback Machine in September 2026 and sit on a review page so you can decide which ones come back.

## Where to go

Labs. tiffanywoodyoga.com/posts (the page is not linked from the site and search engines are told to ignore it; with the switch off it rests under a Maintenance Tool label, as the blog editor does). Each row is one recovered post: a **Keep** checkbox, **Demand**, the title, and **Recs**. A kept post's title opens it in the Class Plans blog editor (below); any other title opens the archived original in the Wayback Machine, and kept posts keep a small 'archived page' link for comparison.

## Demand and recs

The list is sorted by **Demand**: how many people still arrived at each post's old address in the last twelve months, #1 the most. Those old addresses still get traffic from search engines and old links, so the posts at the top are the ones worth deciding on first. Only about 37 posts had any visitors in a year; the rest show none and follow, newest first. The counts refresh every Sunday.

**Recs** is a recommendation from an AI read of each post, shown on the row: **keep** (evergreen teaching), **mixed** (a good core inside a dated wrapper; the note says what to trim), **thin** (under about 150 words, or mostly a video or image), **dated** (tied to a moment that has passed). It is advice, not an order; your call stands over it.

## What a tick does

Ticking Keep saves your choice, and only that. Nothing is published from this page. When you are done, JP takes every ticked post, formats it for the current site, and puts it back into the blog in one batch, a few at a time so search engines take them in calmly. Where an old address still gets visitors, it is pointed at the post's new home.

## The blog as files

The posts you ticked Keep were cleaned up on 2026-09-08: the scrape damage removed, headings and lists put right, the original publish date recovered from the old page, and one picture attached to each (the post's own picture where the archive still had it, otherwise a related photo under a licence that allows it, credited under the picture). Together with the 25 posts already on the site, brought in the same day in the same format, they live as one file per post, and every one has a preview page at tiffanywoodyoga.com/blog_post_preview/<slug>/ inside the site's own page design. Nothing on the site links to a preview and search engines are told to stay out. The recovered original is never touched.

## Editing a post

Labs. The blog editor is part of Class Plans: the Blogs button sits in the Labs menu on the calendar, the small column centred on the right edge under a Labs label, and opens classes.tiffanywoodyoga.com/blog/ (the same login as the rest of Class Plans, so nobody without it can write a post). It lists every post file newest first with its date, status, author and picture: published (the 25 on the site), pending (recovered, waiting for review) and draft (new). New, at /blog/new, starts a post from a title, a date and an author and opens it in the editor at /blog/edit. In the editor the title, date, author, summary, picture description and body are editable, a picture can be added or replaced (JPEG, PNG or WEBP, at least 600 px wide), and Save keeps every version and rewrites the preview at once. Preview opens the preview page; View, on a published post, opens the page visitors see. Publish puts a post on the site: its page appears at tiffanywoodyoga.com/<its slug>/, it sorts into the blog by its date, and its old address from before WordPress, if it had one, sends visitors to the new page. The site rebuilds within about five minutes of the click, and the editor says so while that is on its way. Unpublish takes it back off the same way. Editing a post that is already published updates its preview at once and the live page on the next publish of any post. With the switch off the Blogs button stays, dimmed and inactive, every blog page rests under a Maintenance Tool label, and the posts and the published site stay as they are.

Each post can carry one tag, **Evergreen** or **Dated**, set with the two buttons beside Publish in the editor and shown on the list page, which can be filtered to either. Evergreen means the post reads right at any time of year and can be shared whenever. Dated means it names a month, a season, a moon, a holiday or an event, so it is shared when that comes around. All 89 posts were tagged on 2026-09-13 (68 evergreen, 21 dated, every dated one seasonal). A tag can be flipped any time, and it never appears on the site.

## What this will not do

- It will not publish anything by itself. A pending post is not in the blog and has no address a visitor can find.
- It will not change the recovered original or its date. Editing writes your own copy beside it.
- It will not change the live site the instant you click Publish: the site is rebuilt and swapped as a whole, which takes up to about five minutes, and an edit to an already published post reaches the live page on the next publish.
- It has no deadline. Nothing else waits on it.
