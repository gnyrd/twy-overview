# How do I review the old blog posts?

The site before WordPress (2015 to 2023) carried about 127 blog posts that never made the move. They were recovered from the Wayback Machine in September 2026 and sit on a review page so you can decide which ones come back.

## Where to go

tiffanywoodyoga.com/posts (the page is not linked from the site and search engines are told to ignore it). Each row is one recovered post: a **Keep** checkbox, **Demand**, the title (click it to read and edit the post), and **Recs**. A small 'archived page' link under the title opens the original in the Wayback Machine.

## Demand and recs

The list is sorted by **Demand**: how many people still arrived at each post's old address in the last twelve months, #1 the most. Those old addresses still get traffic from search engines and old links, so the posts at the top are the ones worth deciding on first. Only about 37 posts had any visitors in a year; the rest show none and follow, newest first. The counts refresh every Sunday.

**Recs** is a recommendation from an AI read of each post, shown on the row: **keep** (evergreen teaching), **mixed** (a good core inside a dated wrapper; the note says what to trim), **thin** (under about 150 words, or mostly a video or image), **dated** (tied to a moment that has passed). It is advice, not an order; your call stands over it.

## What a tick does

Ticking Keep saves your choice, and only that. Nothing is published from this page. When you are done, JP takes every ticked post, formats it for the current site, and puts it back into the blog in one batch, a few at a time so search engines take them in calmly. Where an old address still gets visitors, it is pointed at the post's new home.

## Editing a post

Click a title and the post opens in a plain editor: the title in one box, the body in another, and a Save button. Change what you like and save. The recovered original is never touched; your changes go to their own copy, every save is kept, and the list marks the post **edited**. The post keeps its original date (shown at the top of the editor) no matter what you change in the text. When a post you ticked comes back to the blog, your edited copy is the one that comes back. Read the archived page opens the original as it looked, for comparison.

## What this will not do

- It will not publish anything by itself.
- It will not change the recovered original or its date. Editing writes your own copy beside it.
- It has no deadline. Nothing else waits on it.
